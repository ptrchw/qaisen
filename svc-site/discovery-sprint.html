Design Sprint

From a known problem to a tested solution, in four days

Based on the proven Google Ventures sprint method, condensed to four days so your decision maker only needs to be in the room for the first two.

  1. Prep

    A sprint brief agreed with you, a short call with the decision maker, and five users recruited for Day 4.

    Week 1

  2. Day 1: Map and sketch

    Agree the long-term goal, map the problem with your experts, then everyone sketches a solution on their own.

    Week 1

  3. Day 2: Decide and storyboard

    Vote on the strongest ideas, the decision maker makes the call, and the team storyboards the prototype.

    Week 1

  4. Day 3: Prototype GenAI

    I build a realistic prototype with AI assistance, then AI stand-in users pre-test it overnight so the obvious issues are fixed before Day 4.

    Week 2

  5. Day 4: Test with five users

    Five real users try the prototype while your team watches. We end the day agreeing what worked and what to do next.

    Week 2

  6. Summary

    A short report of the findings and the agreed next steps, so the decision holds after the sprint ends.

    Within 3 days

The journey

From noise to a clear decision, in five weeks

The Sprint follows the natural shape of design: wide and messy at the start, focused by the end. Each stage has a fixed slot in the schedule, so the uncertainty never runs open-ended.

Weeks 1–2 · Research & synthesis

Make sense of the noise

Framing workshop, five user interviews, a competitor scan and synthesis into insights and opportunity areas.

Week 3 · Concept & prototype

Choose a direction

Concept workshop, a clickable AI-built prototype and a synthetic-user pre-test to iron out the obvious issues.

Weeks 4–5 · Clarity

Decide with evidence

Testing with five real users, then a clear go, pivot or stop recommendation in the playback session.

How the Discovery Sprint runs

Is AI the right tool for this, and will people actually use it? The Discovery Sprint takes you from assumptions to a tested prototype and a clear recommendation in about five weeks.

GenAI handles the heavy lifting at every stage, so more of your budget goes into judgement: what the findings mean and what you should do next. I review every output, and your team makes every decision.

Participants for both rounds of research are recruited at the start, so there's no second wait halfway through.

  1. Framing workshop

    A half-day with your stakeholders to agree goals, assumptions, the problem statement and what success looks like.

    Week 1

  2. User interviews

    Five interviews of 45–60 minutes, with an interview guide and notes.

    Weeks 1–2

  3. Competitor scan

    How three competitors or alternatives solve the same problem today.

    Week 2

  4. Synthesis

    Key insights, a journey map and the main opportunity areas.

    Week 2

  5. Concept workshop

    A half-day to generate ideas and choose one concept to prototype.

    Week 3

  6. Clickable prototype

    A mid-fidelity prototype of the chosen concept, up to about eight screens, built quickly with AI assistance.

    Week 3

  7. AI pre-test with synthetic users Added step

    Personas built from your interviews work through the prototype first, so obvious problems are fixed before real people see it.

    Week 3

  8. Concept testing

    Five real users try the prototype, followed by analysis.

    Week 4

  9. Recommendation and playback

    The final report and a one-hour session with your team.

    Weeks 4–5

Interviews are recorded only with written consent. Notes are anonymised before any AI tool processes them, and nothing is used to train AI models.

What makes it faster

The AI pre-test

Most discovery projects put the prototype straight in front of real customers. I add one step first: AI stand-in users, built from your interview findings, try it out and flag the obvious problems, and I fix them before anyone else sees it. So when your customers test it, their time goes on what only they can tell you. The AI never replaces real users. It makes their time count.

Grounded in research

The synthetic personas come from what your five real interviews revealed, not from guesswork.

Same tasks as real testing

Each persona works through the planned tasks and explains its reasoning as it goes.

Real users focus on what matters

With confusing labels and dead ends already fixed, real sessions can focus on whether people want it and trust it.

For SMEs without a product team

Evidence before the expensive part starts

An SME can't afford to build the wrong thing, and rarely has a product team to find out first. A Sprint gives you real user evidence at the point where changing direction still costs little.

Before you commit the budget

Test a new product or service idea with real users before it goes into the business case, so you and your partners commit on evidence, not assumptions.

Before you replace a system

See how your staff and customers actually use today's tools and where they let them down, so the new one doesn't rebuild old problems at a new price.

Before you scale a GenAI use case

Check that staff or customers want the AI feature, understand it and trust it, before you roll it out across the business.

Before you brief a vendor

Hand your developer or agency a tested prototype and clear requirements, so you pay for fewer changes mid-build.
