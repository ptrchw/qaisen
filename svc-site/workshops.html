Why invest in your team

GenAI capability that stays in-house

Most teams already have access to GenAI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Far fewer know how to turn them into better products and services. These workshops close that gap, so your team is ready to use them to their full potential and your organisation owns the capability instead of renting it.

Skills your people keep

Your team learns the methods and the tools by doing, so the know-how stays with you instead of leaving with an outside consultant.

GenAI used with judgement

They learn where it speeds up research, ideation and prototyping, where human insight must lead, and how to use it responsibly with company data.

A proven framework, users at the centre

Every exercise follows a structured design thinking and innovation framework that starts from real user needs, so the technology strengthens human-centred thinking rather than replacing it.

Limited to 10 participants, so every team gets hands-on support.

Two teams of five is the right size for building and testing prototypes together. A private, tailored workshop works out at about S$450 per person for a single day.

Three formats

Each one builds on the one before. Choose by how far you want your team to get: from ideas, to a prototype, to a tested concept ready to take to management.

Good to know

More than 10 people?

Run a second cohort in the same month at a reduced rate. The material is already tailored, so you pay mainly for delivery time. Ask about it on our call.

Which GenAI and prototyping tools do we need?

We'll agree this before the workshop. Your team can use licences you already have or the free tiers of common tools. I'll also check your policy on putting company data into GenAI tools, and design the exercises around it.

Is the content tailored to us?

Yes. Exercises use your own products, customers and challenges, agreed with you beforehand.

Is it SkillsFuture-funded?

Not at the moment. SkillsFuture funding only applies to courses run by approved training providers.

What happens after the workshop?

If a prototype from the workshop looks promising, a Discovery Sprint is the natural next step to test it properly with real users.
